The title of this talk is the nature of post-enlightenment hegemonies.

So, what did hegemonies look like in antiquity? There would be one leader of a country, with ultimate power over everyone. He would broker power to other people who would then delegate further. The leader had to have a monopoly on violence or someone could come and use the tactic of 'divide and conquer', and it would become more of a jungle than a society.

In Foucault's seminal work, discipline and punish, he posits that to understand the nature of power, we must look at a society's prisons and criminal judicial system. In antiquity, there would be mortal consequences for disobeying the law. In the Code of Hammurabi, it stipulated that if an architect's building collapses, he would be sentenced to death publicly. Back then, punishments were disproportionate to the crime. So do we think a country's criminal judicial system is to get justice? It has nothing to do with justice. It has everything to do with order and control. People think that this is inefficient and many people are unfairly treated. Foucault would say that it is very efficient for the same reason. However, power has evolved to something quite different in western liberal societies.

With the onset of post-enlightenment age liberal democracies, human rights and other such fantastical ideas, power has evolved to self-regulation and normal people enforcing on themselves and others by acquiescing. Bentham's panopticon is the embodiment of modern discipline.

![Plan of Jeremy Bentham's Panopticon prison, drawn by Willey Reveley, 1791](/writing/panopticon.jpg) It is essentially, a prison in a circular shape with a tower in the middle. The tower has mirrors of which the guards can see into every cell but the prisoners cannot see anything inside the tower. They cannot observe how, when or why the guards observe, and so they must assume that the guards are surveilling them at all times. You must be wondering why I am talking about innovation in prison design, really the point I am making is that power has extrapolated this idea to society as a whole. They decide social norms because they decide what is criminal. They then make you think you have a choice, and think that you thought of the idea yourself. You then, through what Foucault calls normalisation, have everyone enforce these ideas on each other by the distinction between normal and abnormal people. A good example of this is homosexuals in the 20th century. Before 1967, it was illegal to be homosexual and this was reflected in how the general public saw gay acts as wrong whereas today it is widely accepted, although they still do face troubles. The most important feature is that you always act on the assumption that you are being watched.

What does domestic power look like today? Unaccountable and private. When King Charles I tried to put the power of the Bank of England in his own control, he got the English Civil War wherein the merchants essentially waged war on the monarchy. In its conclusion, he lost his head and Oliver Cromwell instituted Liberalism. What he was fighting for, was effectively the power to print money. The Bank of England was privately owned and as long as the Pound Sterling was widely used across the world, which it was, they would be able to print more of it. The cycle being, win wars, get everyone to use the sterling, and pay off debt from the war with the strength of the sterling. This made the British Empire one of the most formidable empires in modern history. The central bank also had another role, to consolidate power inside Britain. Have you ever wondered what inflation actually is. It is a tax. All that new wealth means, is finding more productive processes, to make things cheaper. While cotton used to be highly valued in trade, now it is very cheap due to processes becoming much more productive. If things are getting cheaper in real terms, due to new wealth being created, how are they getting more expensive in nominal terms. This is through loans and interest, and the printing of money. Most money is printed by commercial and investment banks through the credit in fractional reserve banking. However, when there is a run on the bank, the bank cannot create the underlying money from nowhere, and that is where the central bank comes in. Today we call it quantitative easing, or the repo market. So if the government and corporations can print money, why taxes? Taxes create demand for the currency to grant the government the ability to print. Taxes don't just pay for things, in fact they never even break even with the tax receipt, it is just to keep the currency strong in modern monetary theory. Really, central banks create money everyday and use it to inflate asset prices. It is your guess who is the beneficiary of that money. We do not know who is powerful, and there is no one we can blame for the ills in society.

The American Empire is the most significant example of geopolitical power, however. Through the Bretton Woods agreement, the world relied on the USA's gold standard. This was a dollars for gold agreement with the world, so that everyone was convinced that the dollar had value. After the Vietnam war, however, the world was suspicious of the USA's unaccountable and unauditable federal reserve (their central bank). Essentially, they uncovered the scam which was that the USA had much less gold than there was dollars in circulation. Nixon famously closed the gold window in 1971 and the dollar was no longer pegged to gold. They needed to find another way to keep this world reserve currency status, to keep their power to print. Their idea was genius. US diplomat, Henry Kissinger managed to convince King Faisal of Saudi Arabia of the oil for dollars agreement, more commonly known as the petrodollar. This agreement which was written into Saudi law, stipulated that all oil was to be sold in dollars, and that some excess dollars would be used to purchase treasury bonds, which essentially meant lending the USA back these dollars for some interest payments. This now meant that every country had to hold huge amounts of dollars if they wanted to use oil, which every country relied on for energy. This kept the demand for the dollar high, and allowed the USA to be lent money from all over the world to subsidise their huge military, only to be paid back with printed money. And if you didn't sell your oil in dollars, you would end up like Iraq or Libya, or current day Venezuela. Just like what Foucault said, countries do not want to be abnormal and as a result every country who obeys and lends the USA money and power, are paying for their own subjugation to the USA.

Robert Mugabe tried a monetary financing in Zimbabwe, and he got a hyperinflation. The UK is currently doing monetary financing and we have no problems. This is because of our reserve currency status. Two Bretton Woods institutions were instrumental in preserving the world order, or in other words, letting the USA get a free lunch. The IMF and World Bank would act to put third world countries in debt, mainly Africa. The conditionalities on these loans however had dire consequences. Debt doesn't sound morally deplorable, and that's the main advantage in modern day colonialism. You must be thinking, its their fault for not paying their debt. However, while you are paying off your debt, the bank is not allowed to tell you how to run your life and spend your money, only to pay your debt. If they told you that if you default, we can enslave you and your children until the debt is paid, while harvesting your organs, then it would become morally deplorable. And this is essentially what they do. They prevent them from having functional governments, prevent them from having free education, free healthcare, from eating their own food. Instead they have to sell their food to richer countries in the Western Hemisphere, and rely on imports to survive. Fly over Africa and you'll see the richest countries on earth, but they're the opposite inside and this is by no coincidence.

And this is what power looks like today. Pre-modern colonialism had a military presence, and an accountable monarch. Accountable meaning, you can see their face and point towards who is in control. Now colonialism is debt trap diplomacy. You only see a coup d'etat in dictatorships, not liberal democracies. And so all you can do today is be aware of power, and try to ignore liberal dogma where you can.
